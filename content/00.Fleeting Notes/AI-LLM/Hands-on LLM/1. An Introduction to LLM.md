> **Language AI turned text into numbers in stages: count words (bag-of-words), embed meaning (word2vec), let tokens attend to each other (attention), then split into encoder-only models that understand and decoder-only models that generate.**
> 
> - Bag-of-words ignores word order; word2vec captures similarity but gives every word one static embedding, so "bank" never changes with context.
> - Attention escapes the fixed-length context bottleneck, and [[Attention is All You Need (Transformers)]] turned it into the architecture behind LLMs.

Language is tricky concept for computers, Text is unstructured and loses its meaning when reprented by zeros and ones.
As a result, throughout the history of Language AI, there has been a large focus on representing language in a structured manner so that it can more easily be used by computers.

### Bag-of-Words

Bag-of-Words is a method for representing unstructred text. it represents a document by **counting how many times each word appears**, while **ignoring grammar, word order, and sentence structure**.

- First step of the bag-of words model is *tokenization*.
- The Process of splitting up the sentences into individual words or subwords (tokens).
	- The most common method for tokenization is by splitting on a whitespace to create individual words. 
		![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784988979907.webp]]
- After tokenization, we combine all unique words from each sentence to create a vocabulary that we can use to represent the sentences.
	![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784989029775.webp]]
- Using our vocabulary, we simply count how often a word in each sentence appears, quite literally creating a bag of words. As a result, a bag-of-words model aims to create representations of text in the form of numbers, also called vectors or vector representations.
	![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784989195442.webp]]

Example Applications: Spam Email Detection, Text Mining
#### Disadvantages

- Ignores word order and context.
- Cannot capture the meaning of words or relationships between them.
- Produces large, sparse vectors when the vocabulary is large.
- Treats all words as independent, even though many are related.
## Word2Vec

> [!video]- The Illustrated Word2vec - A Gentle Intro to Word Embeddings in Machine Learning
> ![The Illustrated Word2vec - A Gentle Intro to Word Embeddings in Machine Learning - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ISPId9Lhc1g)

Bag-of-Words approach ignores the semantic nature or meaning of text.
word2vec was one of the first successful attempts at capturing the meaning of text in embeddings. It learns semantic relationships between words, allowing similar words to have similar vector representations.

> [!info]- Word2vec Paper
> ![[Word2vec - Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space.pdf]]

To generate these semantic representations, word2vec leverages neural networks. These networks consist of interconnected layers of nodes that process information.
[[1. Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs)|Neural Networks]] can have many layers where each connection has a certain weight depending on the input. *These [[Model Weight#Introduction|Weights]] are often referred to as the parameters of the model.*

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784992454026.webp]]

Using these neural networks, word2vec generates word embeddings by looking at which other words they tend to appear next to in a given sentence.

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784992625021.webp]]

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784992638205.webp]]

> The resulting embeddings capture the meaning of words but what exactly does that mean? 
> To illustrate this phenomenon, let’s somewhat oversimplify and imagine we have embeddings of several words, namely “apple” and “baby.” Embeddings attempt to capture meaning by representing the properties of words. For instance, the word “baby” might score high on the properties “newborn” and “human” while the word “apple” scores low on these properties.

Embeddings are tremendously helpful as they allow us to measure the semantic similarity between two words. Using various distance metrics, we can judge how close one word is to another.

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784992802622.webp]]

- [[#Word2Vec|Word2vec]] - Generates embeddings for words only.

## Encoding and Decoding Context with Attention

The training process of word2vec creates static, downloadable representations of words. For instance, the word “bank” will always have the same embedding regardless of the context in which it is used.

A step in encoding this text was achieved through [[FNN and RNN]].

To do so, these RNNs are used for two tasks, *encoding* or representing an input sentence and *decoding* or generating an output sentence.
![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784996918729.webp]]
### Autoregressive Generation

Each step in this architecture is autoregressive. When generating the next word, this architecture needs to consume all previously generated words.

**Autoregressive** means **predicting the next item based on the previous items in a sequence**.

In Artificial Intelligence and Natural Language Processing (NLP), an **autoregressive model** generates text **one token (word or subword) at a time**, using all the tokens it has already generated as context.

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784997351960.webp]]
### Context Embedding

Using **Word2Vec** embeddings, the input sequence is converted into a **context embedding**, which represents the overall meaning of the entire sequence.
![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784997862634.webp]]
### Attention

However, because the entire input is compressed into a single fixed-length context embedding, it becomes difficult to capture all the information in longer sentences. This can lead to the loss of important contextual details, reducing the model's performance on long sequences.

Attention allows a model to focus on parts of the input sequence that are relevant to one another (“attend” to each other) and amplify their signal,
Attention selectively determines which words are most important in a given sentence.

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1784997964675.webp]]

By adding these attention mechanisms to the decoder step, the RNN can generate signals for each input word in the sequence related to the potential output.

The true power of attention, and what drives the amazing abilities of large lan‐
guage models, was first explored in the well-known [[Attention is All You Need (Transformers)]] paper.

![[Attention is All You Need (Transformers)#Introduction]]
## Representation Models : Encoder-Only Models

**Representation models** are **encoder-only Transformer models** designed to **understand and represent language**, rather than generate text.
Their main output is **embeddings (vector representations)** that capture the meaning and context of text.
The most famous representation model is **BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers)**, introduced in **2018**.

![[Encoder-Only Transformers (BERT)]]
## Generative Models: Decoder-Only Models

- **Generative models** are **decoder-only Transformer models** designed to **generate text**.
- Unlike [[#BERT]], which focuses on understanding language, GPT predicts the **next token** in a sequence to produce coherent text.
- The first GPT model, **GPT-1 (Generative Pre-trained Transformer)**, was introduced in **2018**.
![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1785047758616.webp]]

==The original Transformer contains both an encoder and a decoder. For text generation, only the decoder is needed. GPT removes the encoder and uses **stacked decoder blocks** to generate text.==
### How it Works ?

GPT generates text **autoregressively**, meaning it predicts **one token at a time**.

> [!example]
> **Prompt:** I love
> Prediction: AI
> The input now becomes: I love AI
> Next prediction: because
> 
> This process continues until the response is complete.
### Training Data

GPT-1 was trained on: **7,000 books**, **Common Crawl** (a large collection of web pages)

Through training, GPT learned: Grammar, Vocabulary, Writing styles, General language patterns
### Model Parameters

A **parameter** is a numerical value learned during training that stores the model's knowledge.
Generally:
- **More parameters → Greater learning capacity → Better performance** (assuming sufficient data and training).
![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1785048234098.webp]]
## Representation Models vs Generative Models

|Representation Models (BERT)|Generative Models (GPT)|
|---|---|
|Encoder-only|Decoder-only|
|Understand language|Generate language|
|Produce embeddings|Produce text|
|Bidirectional|Autoregressive (left-to-right)|
|Best for classification, search, clustering|Best for chatbots, summarisation, translation, code generation|
# Large Language Models (LLMs)

Large decoder-only GPT models are commonly called **Large Language Models (LLMs)**.

They can perform tasks such as:
- Answering questions
- Writing essays
- Summarising documents
- Translating languages
- Generating code
- Holding conversations

Although "LLM" often refers to decoder-only models like GPT, the term can also include large encoder-only models such as BERT.
## From Completion Models to Chat Models

Originally, GPT was trained as a **text completion model**.

Example:
Prompt: The capital of France is
Output: Paris.

Later, GPT models were **fine-tuned** to follow instructions and engage in conversations.

Example:
**User:** Explain photosynthesis.
**GPT:** Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert sunlight into energy...

This fine-tuning transformed GPT into **instruction-following** and **chat** models.

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1785048507581.webp]]
## Context Window (Context Length)

The **context window** is the maximum number of **tokens** the model can process at one time.

It includes:
- The user's prompt.
- Previous conversation or text.
- Tokens the model has already generated.

> [!example]
> If a model has a **4,000-token context window**, it can consider approximately 4,000 tokens of text at once. As the model generates new tokens, they also occupy part of this context window.

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1785048543015.webp]]

A larger context window allows the model to:
- Read longer documents.
- Maintain longer conversations.
- Remember more previous information.
- *Work alongside retrieval when relevant context must be selected from external knowledge.*
## The Training Paradigm of LLM

Creating LLMs, in contrast, typically consists of at least two steps:

![[1. An Introduction to LLM-1785049061101.webp]]

### 1. Language Modeling (Pretraining)

- **Pretraining** is the first and most computationally expensive stage of training an LLM.
- The model is trained on **massive amounts of text** (e.g., internet articles, books, websites).
- During this stage, it learns:
    - Grammar
    - Vocabulary
    - Context
    - Language patterns
- The objective is to **predict the next word/token** in a sequence.
- The resulting model is called a **foundation model** or **base model**.
- A base model has general language knowledge but **does not naturally follow instructions**.
### 2. Fine-Tuning (Post-Training)

- [[6. Fine Tuning LLMs|Fine-tuning]] is the process of further training a pretrained model on a **specific task or dataset**.
- It adapts the base model to perform particular tasks or behaviours, such as:
    - Text classification
    - Sentiment analysis
    - Question answering
    - Instruction following
    - Chatbots
- [[6. Fine Tuning LLMs|Fine-tuning]] is much **faster and less resource-intensive** than pretraining because it builds on an already trained model.
- This makes it practical for organisations and individuals to customise foundation models without repeating the expensive pretraining process.
